## How to Collect Testimonials Guide

### When to Ask for Testimonials

**Timing is everything. Ask when:**
1. Customer just achieved a win (high engagement, good reviews collected)
2. They've mentioned positive results in support tickets
3. After they renew their license (indicates satisfaction)
4. They leave a 5-star review on WordPress.org
5. They share positive experience on social media

### Who to Ask

**Prioritize customers with:**
- Clear, measurable results (increased engagement, time saved)
- Interesting use cases (unique industries, creative implementations)
- Different site types (covers all buyer personas)
- Various pricing tiers (single, 5-site, unlimited)
- Upgraded from free to Pro (shows value realization)

### How to Ask

**Email Template:**

```
Subject: Quick question - how's Member Reviews working for [Site Name]?

Hi [Name],

I noticed [specific observation - e.g., "you have 200+ reviews collected" or "you renewed your license"].

Would love to hear how BuddyPress Member Reviews is working for your community. Specifically:

1. What problem were you trying to solve?
2. How has the plugin helped?
3. Any specific results? (engagement increase, time saved, etc.)

If you're happy to share, I'd love to feature your experience as a case study to help other community managers.

In return, I can offer:
- Link to your site from our showcase
- Priority support for 6 months
- Early access to new features

Takes about 10 minutes. Interested?

Thanks,
[Your Name]
```

### Questions to Ask

**For Quote Testimonials:**
1. What was your biggest challenge before using Member Reviews?
2. Why did you choose our plugin over alternatives?
3. What's the most valuable feature for your community?
4. What results have you seen? (numbers if possible)
5. Would you recommend it? To whom?

**For Case Studies:**
1. Describe your site and community size
2. What problem were you solving?
3. What solution did you try before this?
4. How did you implement Member Reviews?
5. What were the results after 30/60/90 days?
6. What's the ROI or value you've gained?
7. Any unexpected benefits?
8. What would you tell someone considering the plugin?

### Making It Easy

**Reduce friction:**
- Offer to write the testimonial based on their email answers (they just approve)
- Record a 10-minute video call and transcribe it
- Pull quotes from positive support tickets (with permission)
- Use their WordPress.org review as a starting point

### What Makes a Great Testimonial

**Include:**
- Specific results (numbers, percentages, time saved)
- Before/after comparison
- Specific feature mentioned
- Customer's role and site type (credibility)
- Emotional outcome (satisfied members, less stress)

**Avoid:**
- Generic praise ("great plugin!")
- No context about their use case
- Vague benefits ("improved our site")
- Too long (edit to 2-3 sentences for quotes)

### Where to Use Testimonials

**Placement strategy:**
1. Homepage - 3 rotating quotes from different personas
2. Pricing page - ROI-focused testimonials near upgrade CTA
3. Feature pages - Testimonials highlighting specific features
4. Checkout - Trust signals from similar customers
5. Email campaigns - Social proof for hesitant leads
6. Case study library - Full stories with metrics

### Permission and Attribution

**Always:**
- Get written permission to use testimonials
- Allow customer to review final quote/case study
- Offer anonymity option (role/industry only, no name)
- Link to their site (if they want promotion)
- Ask if they want to be contacted by potential customers
- Respect GDPR/privacy for European customers

### Incentives That Work

**Offer value, not bribes:**
- Extended support period
- Feature showcase on your site (promotion for them)
- Early beta access to new features
- Free design/setup consultation
- Discount on next renewal (small, not main motivator)
- Co-marketing opportunities (guest blog, webinar)

### Follow-Up

**After collecting testimonial:**
1. Thank them promptly
2. Show them where it's featured
3. Deliver promised incentives
4. Share the case study on social (tag them)
5. Check in quarterly for updated results
6. Ask for referrals to similar customers

### Red Flags to Avoid

**Don't use testimonials that:**
- Sound too good to be true (fake-sounding)
- Can't be verified (made-up names, fake sites)
- Are from obvious competitors
- Violate customer confidentiality
- Make claims about results you can't guarantee
- Are overly promotional without substance

### Testimonial Database

**Track in spreadsheet:**
- Customer name, role, site URL
- Use case and persona type
- Key features they love
- Specific results/metrics
- Quote (short and long versions)
- Permission status and date
- Where it's currently used
- Last updated date

This lets you rotate testimonials and match the right social proof to each marketing context.
